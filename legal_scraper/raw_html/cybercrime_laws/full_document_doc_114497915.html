                                                COMMON ORDER

Crl.O.P.No.17269 of 2015 has been filed by the accused No.2 to quash the proceedings against him in C.C.No.2740 of 2015 on the file of the X Metropolitan Magistrate, Saidapet, Chennai.

Crl.O.P.No.21603 of 2015 has been filed by the accused No.1 to quash the proceedings against him in C.C.No.2740 of 2015 on the file of the X Metropolitan Magistrate, Saidapet, Chennai.

2. The respondent herein is a practicing advocate in Chennai. She lodged a complaint before the Commissioner of Police, Chennai, on 27.03.2015 stating that the accused Balaji Kumar is an employee of Tata Consultancy Services, Mumbai. She further stated that the said Balaji Kumar has endorsed the view of one Adiraman who has depicted women in scandalize manner by posting a cartoon which captioned 'before marriage' and 'after marriage' wherein he has scandalously mentioned that girls are all shaved by father prior to marriage and by husband after marriage which can be seen by the photographs annexed with the said complaint. She further stated that the said Balaji Kumar has lowered the image of women among the right thinking and reasonable people in the society and thereby committed an offence which is defamatory and scandalous and requested to take action against him.

3. The said complaint was forwarded to the Inspector of Police, Cyber Crime Cell, Central Crime Branch, Chennai–7. The Inspector of Police, http://www.judis.nic.in 3 Cyber Crime Cell, has conducted an enquiry and closed the said complaint and dropped further action on the said complaint and informed the same to the respondent on 15.04.2015. In the meanwhile, the respondent has filed a petition in Crl.O.P.No.11292 of 2015 before this court to direct the Inspector of Police, Cyber Crime, to register an FIR on the basis of the complaint given by her dated 27.03.2015. This court by the order dated 29.04.2015, taking into consideration that the Inspector of Police, Cyber Crime, has closed the said complaint, closed the aforesaid petition with liberty to the respondent herein to work out her remedies in the manner known to law. Thereafter, the respondent has filed a private complaint before the XI Metropolitan Magistrate, Saidapet, Chennai, alleging that the Accused Nos.1 and 2 have committed offence against women as envisaged under Section 67 of Information Technology Act, Section 354 (D) and 509 of IPC and hence, they should be tried and punished for the aforesaid offences.

4. The learned XI Metropolitan Magistrate, after recording sworn statement of the respondent herein under Section 200 of Cr.P.C., and perusing the complaint and other materials enclosed with the complaint, has taken case on file in C.C.No.2740 of 2015 and issued summons to the accused Nos.1 and 2. The accused Nos.1 and 2, after receipt of the summons, filed the present petitions under Section 482 Cr.P.C., to quash the proceedings against them in the aforesaid C.C. http://www.judis.nic.in

5. The learned counsel for the petitioners has submitted that no prima 4 facie case is made out against the petitioners under Section 67 of the Information Technology Act and under Sections 354 D and 509 IPC. They further submitted that since Uniform Resource Locator (URL) which is the address of a web page of the disputed content was not found on the face- book accounts of Balaji Kumar, the Inspector of Police, Cyber Crime, has dropped further action on the complaint filed by the respondent. They further submitted that the Hon'ble Supreme Court has already struck down Section 66-A of the Information Technology Act as unconstitutional and hence, prosecution cannot be launched against the petitioners herein. They further submitted that the respondent herein made inconsistent statement in the complaint lodged before the police, in the complaint filed before the XI Metropolitan Magistrate and in the sworn statement and therefore, the allegations made by the respondent/complainant are so absurd and inherently improbable on the basis of which no prudent person can ever reach a just conclusion that there is sufficient ground for proceeding against the petitioners.

6. The learned counsel for the petitioners further submitted that the respondent has not produced any material to show that the first accused has posted the alleged photo in his face-book and the second accused gave like. They further submitted that even assuming that the first accused has posted the said photo in his face-book and the second accused has given like to the said picture message, it cannot be said that they have committed any offence. They further submitted that the respondent herein is the own sister of the second accused's father-in-law http://www.judis.nic.in 5 and there is a family dispute between them and due to the said dispute, to harass the second accused, she filed a false complaint. They further submitted that since the first accused is also working with the second accused in the software company, the respondent roped the first accused also in the above complaint and therefore, they prayed to quash the proceedings against them. In support of their contentions, they relied upon the decision in Shreya Singhal Union of India (2015) 3 MLJ 162 (SC)

7. Per contra, the respondent/party-in-person has submitted that the first accused has posted the objectionable photo in his face-book account in a public domain wherein it depicts that women in their life is a burden / difficulty / nuisance / annoyance / disturbance / swindlers in the men's life, more particularly, before marriage, they are burden/difficulty/nuisance/annoyance/disturbance/swindler to her father and after marriage, they are burden / difficulty / nuisance / annoyance / disturbance / swindlers to her husband. In other words, the women are shaving their fathers head before marriage and shaving their husbands head after marriage. She further submitted that the second accused gave 'like' for the aforesaid photo and hence they have committed the offence punishable under Section 67 of the Information Technology Act, under Section 354-D and 509 of IPC. She further submitted that inspite of URL number has been sent by her in Whatsapp and also by way of SMS, the Inspector of Police, Cyber Crime, has purposely with a view to help the accused, delayed to check the face-book account of the accused persons http://www.judis.nic.in 6 and taking advantage of the same, the accused persons have deleted the said photos from their face-book account. She further submitted that the photos were posted in the face-book account of the accused persons who depicted women in worst form that too, in the public domain and hence, a prima facie case is made out against them and therefore, she prayed to dismiss the above petitions.

8. In support of the aforesaid contentions, the respondent/party-in- person relied upon the following decisions:

1. Rahul Gandhi Vs.Dr. Subramanian Swamy and another (CRL.MCs.3332/2014) order dated 07.12.2015 of the Delhi High Court
2. State of Haryana and others Vs. CH.Bhajan lal and others (1992) AIR 604 and 1990 SCR Supl (3) 259.

9. The respondent herein has filed a private complaint alleging that on 15.03.2015, she came across a photo in her face-book account wherein it depicted that women in their life is a burden / difficulty / nuisance / annoyance / disturbance / swindlers in men's life, more particularly, before marriage, they are burden to their fathers and after marriage, they are burden to their husbands and also stated that in other words, the women are shaving their fathers head before marriage and shaving their husbands head after marriage and the said photo was posted by the first accused in the face-book account and the second accused gave 'like' for the said photo and hence, they have committed offences punishable under Section 67 of the Information Technology Act, under Section 354-D and 509 of IPC.

10. Section 67 of the Information Technology Act reads thus:-

“ 67. Publishing of information which is obscene in electronic form. -Whoever publishes or transmits or causes to be published in the electronic form, any material which is lascivious or appeals to the prurient interest or if its effect is such as to tend to deprave and corrupt persons who are likely, having regard to all relevant circumstances, to read, see or hear the matter contained or embodied in it, shall be punished on first conviction with imprisonment of either http://www.judis.nic.in description for a term which may extend to five years and 8 with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees and in the event of a second or subsequent conviction with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to ten years and also with fine which may extend to two lakh rupees."

11.Section 354 -D of IPC reads thus:

Stalking (1) Any man who—
1. follows a woman and contacts, or attempts to contact such woman to foster personal interaction repeatedly despite a clear indication of disinterest by such woman; or
2. monitors the use by a woman of the internet, email or any other form of electronic communication,commits the offence of stalking;

Provided that such conduct shall not amount to stalking if the man who pursued it proves that—

1. it was pursued for the purpose of preventing or detecting crime and the man accused of stalking had been entrusted with the responsibility of prevention and detection of crime by the State; or

2. it was pursued under any law or to comply with any condition or requirement imposed by any person under any law; or

3. in the particular circumstances such conduct was reasonable and justified.

(2) Whoever commits the offence of stalking shall be punished on first conviction with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years, and shall also be liable to fine; and be punished on a second or subsequent conviction, with http://www.judis.nic.in imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to 9 five years, and shall also be liable to fine.

1 Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013”

12. Section 509 of IPC reads as follows:-

“509. Word, gesture or act intended to insult the modesty of a woman.—Whoever, intending to insult the modesty of any woman, utters any word, makes any sound or gesture, or exhibits any object, intending that such word or sound shall be heard, or that such gesture or object shall be seen, by such woman, or intrudes upon the privacy of such woman, shall be punished with simple imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year, or with fine, or with both.”

13. In this case, the alleged photo which was said to have been posted in the face book account has been filed by the petitioners along with the typed set of papers. In the said photograph, two captions are mentioned; one is “before marriage” and another one is “after marriage”. In the photograph containing the caption “before marriage”, it appears that about six or seven persons are standing, one person is mentioned as “Boy”; another person is mentioned as “Daughter”; third person is mentioned as “Dad”. In that photograph, the person who is shown as “Dad” has put his hand on the head of the person who is shown as “Daughter”. The person who is shown as 'Boy' is watching the same. In the photograph which is mentioned as “after marriage” one person is mentioned as 'husband' and another person is mentioned as 'wife' and the third person is mentioned as 'uncle'. In that photograph, the person who is mentioned as 'husband' has put one of his hands on the head of the person who is mentioned as http://www.judis.nic.in 10 'wife' and the person who is mentioned as 'uncle' watching the same. From the said photographs, it cannot be said that any lascivious or prurient interest materials are published which tends to deprave and corrupt persons. So, the Provision of Section 67 of the Information Technology Act will not attract. Sections 354-D and 509 of IPC also will not attract.

14. The statements made by the respondent/complainant in the complaint lodged before the police and the complaint filed before the Magistrate and the sworn statement also would show that the complainant herself could not came to just conclusion as to what kind of message has been depicted in the said photographs.

15. In the complaint filed before the police, she has stated as follows:

“ I beg to prefer this complaint to you against one Balaji Kumar S/o.K.Kumar, an employee of Tata Consultancy Services, Mumbai, Maharashtra who has committed an offence against working women in Face Book profile dated

16.03.2015. The said Balaji Kumar has endorsed the view of one Adiraman who has depicted women in Scandalize manner by posting a cartoon which captioned before marriage and after marriage wherein he had scandalously mentioned that girls are all shaved by father prior to marriage and by husband after marriage which can be seen by the photographs annexed with this complaint below.” http://www.judis.nic.in 16. In the complaint filed before the Magistrate, she has stated as 11 follows:

“ The Complainant on 15.03.2015 came across a “Photo” in her face-book account, wherein it depicts that women in their life is a burden / difficulty / nuisance / annoyance / disturbance / swindlers in the men's life, more particularly before marriage they are burden / difficulty / nuisance / annoyance / disturbance / swindlers to her father and after marriage they are burden / difficulty / nuisance / annoyance / disturbance / swindlers to her husband. In other words, the women are shaving their fathers head before marriage and shaving their husbands head after marriage.”

18. From the reading of the aforesaid statement, it is clear that the sworn statement given by the respondent under Section 200 Cr.P.C., is totally against the allegation made in the complaint filed before the Magistrate. In the complaint filed before the Magistrate, she has stated that the photo depicts that women in their life is a burden / difficulty / nuisance / annoyance / disturbance / swindlers before marriage to their fathers and after marriage to their husbands. In other words, the women are shaving their fathers head before marriage and shaving their husband's head after marriage. Whereas in the sworn statement recorded under Section 200 Cr.P.C., she has stated that the aforesaid photo depicts that before marriage, the father was extracting money from her and deprive the income of the working daughter and after marriage, the husband is extracting money from and out of the income of the working wife. So it is clear that the respondent herself is not sure what kind of message has been given by the aforesaid photo.

19. If it is assumed that the aforesaid photo depicts the message as stated by the respondent in her sworn statement, it cannot be said that the said message scandalize the women. On the contrary, it just exposes the exploitation being done by the parents with their working daughters for extracting money and after marriage the husbands extracting money by exploiting their innocent wives. So, it can be taken as a message given to the public for creating awareness. Hence, at no stretch of imagination it http://www.judis.nic.in 13 can be said that the aforesaid message would attract any of the penal provisions of law.

20. In the leading case in the State of Haryana and others Vs. CH.Bhajanlal and others (supra), the Hon'ble Supreme Court by way of illustration stated seven categories of cases where the extraordinary power under Article 226 or inherent power under Section 482 Cr.P.C., can be exercised by the High Court either to prevent abuse of process of any court or otherwise to secure the ends of justice. The said seven categories are extracted hereunder:-

“1. where the allegations made in the First Information Report or the complaint, even if they are taken at their face value and accepted in their entirety do not prima facie constitute any offence or make out a case against the ac- cused;
2. where the allegations in the First Information Report and other materials, if any, accompanying the F.I.R. do not disclose a cognizable offence, justifying an investi- gation by police officers under Section 156(1) of the Code except under an order of a Magistrate within the purview of Section 155(2) of the Code;
3. where the uncontroverted allegations made in the FIR or 'complaint and the evidence collected in support of the same do not disclose the commission of any offence and make out a case against the accused;
4. where the allegations in the FIR do not constitute a cognizable offence but constitute only a non-cognizable offence, no investigation is permitted by a police officer without an order of a Magistrate as contemplated under Section 155(2) of the Code;
5. where the allegations made in the FIR or complaint are so absurd and inherently improbable on the basis of which no prudent person can ever reach a just conclusion that there is sufficient http://www.judis.nic.in 14 ground for proceeding against the accused;
6. where there is an express legal bar engrafted in any of the provisions of the Code or the concerned Act (under which a criminal proceeding is instituted) to the institu- tion and continuance of the proceedings and/or where there is a specific provision in the Code or the concerned Act, providing efficacious redress for the grievance of the aggrieved party;
7. where a criminal proceeding is manifestly attended with mala fide and/or where the proceeding is maliciously instituted with an ulterior motive for wreaking vengeance on the accused and with a view to spite him due to private and personal grudge.”

21. This court is of the view that this case will fall under the category of '5' i.e., where the allegation made in the FIR or complaint are so absurd or inherently improbable on the basis of which, no prudent person can ever reach the just conclusion that there is a sufficient ground for proceeding against the accused. In this case, as already pointed out that the aforesaid photo, according to the respondent, is an objectionable one, but the sworn statement of the respondent would clearly show that it is only a message given to the public for creating awareness. Therefore, it cannot be said that the petitioners herein have committed offence punishable under the aforesaid sections .

22. The other citations relied upon by the respondent are not relevant for this case.

23. The decision relied upon by the petitioners i.e. Shreya Singhal 15 Union of India (2015) 3 MLJ 162 (SC) is relating to struck down of Section 66-A of the Information Technology Act and the said judgment also not relevant to decide this case.

24. For the aforesaid reasons, this court is of the view that the continuance of the proceedings against the petitioners herein would amount to abuse of process of the court and hence, the same is liable to be quashed. Accordingly, these petitions are allowed. Consequently, the connected miscellaneous petitions are closed. The proceedings against the petitioners herein in C.C.No.2740 of 2015 on the file of the learned XI Metropolitan Magistrate, Saidapet, are quashed.
