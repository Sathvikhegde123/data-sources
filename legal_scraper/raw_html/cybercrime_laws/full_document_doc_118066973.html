This petition u/S 482 r/W Sec 407 of Cr.P.C. is preferred against the order dated 04.11.2022 passed in SCATR No. 99/2021 by the learned First Additional Sessions Judge and Special Judge (POCSO) Act, Distt. Dewas.

2. It is stated in the petition that P.S. Nemawar Distt. Dewas registered a missing person report and FIR bearing Crime No. 132/2021 for offence punishable u/S 363 of IPC with regard to missing of Mamta Bai, Divya, Rupali, Pawan and Pooja. After investigation, it was revealed that the accused in furtherance of a conspiracy killed Mamta Bai, Divya, Rupali, Pawan and Pooja on the intervening night of 13/14.05.2021. Nemawar filed final report u/S 173 of Cr.P.C. for offence punishable u/S 363, 302, 201, 120-B, 34 , 364, 376, 376(2)(n) of IPC ; Section 3(2)(v) and 3(2)(va) of SC/ST(Prevention of Atrocities Act); Section 66B and 66C of the Information Technology Act and Section 5L and 6 of Prevention of Children from Sexual offences Act, 2012. 3. Feeling aggrieved by non-transfer of the case to the Court of Special Judge(CBI), Indore, present petition is filed on the following grounds:

1. The learned trial Court at Dewas has no jurisdiction to try the case as the same was investigated by the CBI.
2. The Special Court(CBI), Indore alone has jurisdiction to try the matter.
3. The trial by a Court without jurisdiction would be in nullity.
4. On these grounds, it is requested that the impugned order dated

04.11.2022 passed by the learned First Additional Sessions Judge and Special Judge(POCSO Act), Dewas in SCATR No. 99/2021 be quashed and the case be transferred for trial to Special Court(CBI), Indore.

5. Learned counsel for the petitioner referring to M.P. Rajpatra dated 23.06.2017 and notification No. F. No. 1-1-88-XXI-B(One)-1488-2017 dated 12.05.2017 contends that Special Judge CBI has jurisdiction not only to try the cases relating to offences specified u/S 3 of Prevention of Corruption Act, but also all other offences investigated by the CBI. Thus, the Special Court(CBI), Indore has exclusive jurisdiction to try the cases investigated by the CBI, irrespective of the nature of offence. The matter was investigated v i d e notification dated 11.04.2022 by the CBI and a supplementary chargsheet was filed, therefore, in view of law laid down in the case of Vinay Tyagi Vs. Irshad Ali reported in (2013) 5 SCC 762 , only a special Court constituted for trial of cases investigated by CBI has jurisdiction to try the case. Learned Special Judge (POCSO Act), Dewas committed error in declining to transfer the matter to Special Court, (CBI), Indore.

6. Per contra, learned counsel appearing for the State opposes the application and contends that learned Special Judge, SC/ST(Prevention of Atrocities) Act, Dewas had taken cognizance on the basis of final report submitted by the Investigating Officer, DSP Prashant Bhadoriya, upon the investigation of Crime No. 132/2021 registered at P.S. Nemawar Distt. Dewas. After cognizance by the competent special Court, the matter was further investigated by the CBI and a supplementary final report was filed on 04.11.2022. Since, the cognizance has already been taken by a Court of competent jurisdiction on a final report submitted u/S 173 of Cr.P.C., the supplementary final report would not oust jurisdiction of Special Judge (POCSO Act), Dewas. Further, learned Special Judge(CBI), Indore is not Signature Not Verified Signed by: SEHAR HASEEN Signing time: 25-11-2024 18:54:25 NEUTRAL CITATION NO. 2024:MPHC-IND:32816 4 MCRC-47764-2023 authorized by notification to try the cases relating to offence punishable under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012, whereas, the learned Special Judge(POCSO Act), Dewas by virtue of special designation has jurisdiction to try such offences. Therefore, the learned Special Judge(POCSO Act) has not committed any error in declining transfer of case to Special Judge(CBI), Indore.

7. Heard, rival contentions advanced by the parties and perused the record.

8. The Statement of Objects and Reasons of the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012, reads as under-

4) It is, therefore, proposed to enact a self contained comprehensive legislation inter-alia to provide for protection of children from offences of sexual assault, sexual harassment and pornography with due regard for safeguarding the interest and well being of the child at every stage of the judicial process, incorporating child friendly procedures for reporting, recording of evidence, investigation and trial of offences, procedures for reporting, recording of evidence, investigation and trial of offences and provision for establishment of Special Courts for speedy trial of such offences.

9. The POCSO Act, 2012 provides for designation of Special Courts as under-

28. Designation of Special Courts - (1) For the purposes of providing a speedy trial, the State Government shall in consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court, by notification in the Official Gazette, designate for each district, a Court of Session to be a Special Court to try the offences under the Act:

Provided that if a Court of Session is notified as a children‟s court under the Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005 (4 of 2006) or a Special Court designated for similar purposes under any other law for the time being in force, then, such court shall be deemed to be a Special Court under this section.
(2) While trying an offence under this Act, a Special Court shall also try an offence[other than the offence referred to in sub-

(3) The Special Court constituted under this Act, notwithstanding anything in the Information Technology Act, 200(21 of 2000), shall have jurisdiction to try offences under section 67B of that Act insofar as it relates to publication or transmission of sexually explicit material depicting children in any act, or conduct or manner or facilitates abuse of children online.

10. Thus, the POCSO Act is a special statute and its provisions have an overriding effect in view of Section 42A, which states that the Act shall prevail over other laws in case of inconsistencies. The CBI can investigate cases involving offences punishable under the POCSO Act, but it cannot be a determining factor to confer jurisdiction, unless the Special Court is notified u/S 28 of the Act. The Special Judge (POCSO Act) has an exclusive jurisdiction involving the offence concerning child sexual abuse. The cases involving offence punishable under the POCSO Act must be tried by Special Court designated under the Act for maintaining special procedure aimed at child sensitivity.

11. It is pertinent to note that initially, the investigation was conducted in Crime No. 132/2021 for offences punishable u/S 363, 302, 201, 120-B, 34 , 364, 376, 376(2)(n) of IPC ; Section 3(2)(v) and 3(2)(va) of SC/ST(Prevention of Atrocities Act); Section 66B and 66C of the Information Technology Act and Section 5L and 6 of Prevention of Children from Sexual offences Act, 2012 by the State Police and on completion of investigation, the final report u/S 173 of Cr.P.C. was filed. Thereafter, CBI further investigated the matter and filed supplementary chargsheet against the same 09 accused persons. 12. The Learned Special Judge(POCSO Act), Dewas has taken cognizance of the supplementary final report and proceeded with the trial of the accused, who were already named in the previous final report filed by P.S. Nemawar. Learned Special Judge(POCSO Act), Dewas has committed no error in declining request for transfer of case. Therefore, no case of procedural impropriety or irregularity is made out for invoking the inherent jurisdiction of this Court u/S 482 r/W Sec 407 of Cr.P.C.

13. The petition, sans merit, is hereby dismissed.
